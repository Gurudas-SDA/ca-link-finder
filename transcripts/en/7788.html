The concepts of mental inclination, 'mineness', and so on are sometimes referred to by the word bhakti only due to their connection with it. Those who conceive of themselves as recipients of grace are of two types, because grace itself is of two types: nurturing (poṣaṇa) and compassion (anukampā). Here, nurturing means the giving of bliss by the Lord through His own form and His own qualities. Compassion is the Lord's tender-hearted desire to benefit His servants and others by creating in them a desire for His service, even though He is complete in Himself, thereby bestowing upon them the good fortune of service. Among these two types, some are without a sense of 'mineness' (nirmamāḥ) toward the Lord, and some possess it (samamāḥ). Among them, the jñānī-bhaktas like Śrī Sanaka, who conceive of themselves as objects of bliss through the Lord's aspect as Paramātmā and Para-brahman, are nirmamāḥ. Their lack of 'mineness' toward Him, despite the dissolution of difference, is like the saying: "O Lord, although the distinction between us is removed, I am Yours, but You are not mine. A wave belongs to the ocean, but the ocean never belongs to a wave." There, like seeing the moon, their vision of the Lord brings them pleasure even without 'mineness'. Their favorable disposition here is to be understood through their inclination towards Him, their praises, and so on. Their prīti is called jñāna-bhakti. It is jñāna because it is experienced solely as condensed Brahman. This very state is called śānti, because it is predominated by tranquility (śama). The Lord's statement is: "Śama is the state of the intellect being fixed in Me" [BhP 11.19.36].
