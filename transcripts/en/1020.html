'Yan maithunādi-gṛhamedhi-sukhaṁ hi tucchaṁ kaṇḍūrayor iva karayor iva duḥkha-duḥkham tṛpyanti neha bahu-nā bahu-du... kṛpaṇā bahu-duḥkha-bhājaḥ kaṇḍūtivan manasijaṁ viṣaheta dhīraḥ.'" He said, "The living entities come together, they try to find a partner to enjoy sex life. But this sexual desire is like a skin disease. That means it is a itching of the senses. So when you have a skin disease, you scratch. Then that moment as you scratch, you feel some relief. Then by this scratching, it becomes more inflamed. And it becomes more tender. Tierna. And you scratch, it becomes all sore. 'Incluso si sigues rascándote (..) vas a llegar a tener...' And you cannot stop. So," he said that, "This sexual desire is like a itching disease that the more you try to satisfy it, the more it increases. And simply gives pain. There is no feeling of satisfaction. So (..) this itch, kaṇḍūtivan manasijam, it is in the mind. By the contamination of rajo-guṇa, the mode of passion in the mind, is the itching sensation. But for the sake of trying to satisfy this itch, all types of (..) bondage, that means situations in which one becomes trapped, they accumulate around the living entity. So," he said, "I looked, and I saw everyone trying to enjoy through wealth and (..) sense gratification, but always they get distress. So I realized the desire for wealth and the desire for prestige, the reputation and respect of others was the root cause of bondage. So I decided to give up all these things. And I refused to do any karmic actions. I only absorb myself always in remembering the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa." He said, "In this regard, the bumblebee and the python are my gurus. (..) Pitón. Because the bumblebee is satisfied when he take only a little here and there. And the python, he just lies there in the mud. And if food walks into his mouth, he takes. And if not, he's satisfied. He just stays there. So, I adopted the what is called ajagara-vṛtti, that means the profession of a python. So I just lie here in this forest." So then he remembered Prahlāda Mahārāja's question, "But why are you so fat?" "According to my karma, everything is coming. Some days I have no food. Other days, just a little comes. And some days I eat like a king. But whatever happens, I am satisfied. Sometimes I sleep (..) in the dust. Sometimes I sleep on flowers. Sometimes I sleep on linen sheets. Sometimes on cotton. Sometimes on silk. Sometimes in the palace of a king. What happens, happens. Sometimes a king may come and say, 'Oh, please come and stay in my palace for a few days.' So, because I was journeying through millions of lifetimes trying by force to enjoy, I left this. But still, all according to karma, everything comes without any effort. I know that all the people of the world have different natures. So I never praise them, and I never criticize. Only, I feel goodwill towards everyone. I want that one day, everyone will find their relation with Kṛṣṇa. So, I advise them that the conceptions of duality of good and bad... ...these are two sides of the same coin. They are born from the mental energy. So just like a brāhmaṇa performs a yajña, and he takes the ghee and offers into the fire: svāhā... ...and the ghee is consumed by the fire... ...so in the same way, I take all ideas of good and bad, loss and gain, victory and defeat, and I offer them, svāhā, into the fire of my controlled mind. Finished, no more duality. Then I offer my mind into the fire of my ego. Mind finished. And then, I make oblation of my ego into prakṛti, the unmanifested material nature, svāhā, finished. Prakṛti. And being free from the darkness of (..) the coverings of māyā, then I have darśana of Kṛṣṇa. So, I am passing my time in great bliss."

And then he gave praṇāma to that paramahaṁsa and took the dust from his feet and he went back to the palace. Mission accomplished. So, when you understand this kathā... ...and you apply it to your life... ...then you are situated in sambandha. Understand that you are ātmā, you have no relation with worldly things.
