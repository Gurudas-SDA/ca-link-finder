[00:06:23] There are two contradictory states of consciousness. One is the sākṣitva, when we are aware that I am a spiritual soul, and I am looking at the world, I am looking at this physical body, and I am looking at the mind from the vantage point of ātmā, myself, the soul. Unless and until a person becomes situated in the position of sākṣitva, witnesshood, having the objective vision towards his own relationship with the body and mind, then pure bhakti has not begun. The contradictory position is having a consciousness which is colored or pervaded by the spirit of the viṣaya-bhoga, that means wanting to taste and enjoy the objects of this world. When the consciousness is colored with the, (..) mood of viṣaya-bhoga, enjoyment towards this world, then there is no question of being situated in sākṣitva, the awareness of one's soul and acting on the platform of the soul. So, these two positions, they are contradictory. It is not possible to be situated in the sākṣitva and have the spirit of enjoyment; it is not possible to have the spiritual, (..) the spirit of enjoyment and be aware of your soul's nature. So, they are incompatible conditions. My Gurudeva used to say, "You cannot put two swords in the same scabbard."
