sarva-saṁvādinī : sarva-granthārthaṁ saṅkṣepeṇa darśayann api maṅgalācarati—yasya iti | kvacid api satyaṁ jñānam anantaṁ brahma [tai.u. 2.1.2] ity ādāv api-śabdena tatraiva brahmatvaṁ mukhyam ity ānītam | aṁśakaiḥ līlāvatāra-rūpair guṇāvatāra-rūpaiś ca | pumān puruṣaḥ sarvāntaryāmī paramātmākhyaḥ | ekaṁ śrī-kṛṣṇākhyād anyat | yasyaiva iti tasya bhagavattva-sāmye’pi śrī-kṛṣṇasyaiva svayaṁ-bhagavattvaṁ darśitam | nārāyaṇākhyaṁ rūpaṁ pādottara-khaṇḍādi-pratipādyaḥ parama-vyomākhya-mahā-vaikuṇṭhādhipaḥ śrīpatiḥ | “svayaṁ bhagavān” iti, kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayaṁ [bhā.pu. 1.3.28] iti śrī-bhāgavata-prāmāṇyam iheti sūcitam | śrīr iti tad-avyabhicāriṇī svarūpa-śaktir api darśitā | iha jagati | tat-pāda-bhājāṁ tac-caraṇāravindaṁ bhajatām | prema prīty-atiśayam | vidhattāṁ kurutāṁ prādurbhāvayatv ity arthaḥ ||8||While briefly showing the meaning of the entire work, he also performs an auspicious invocation with the verse 'yasya,' etc. 'kvacid api' refers to scriptures like 'satyaṁ jñānam anantaṁ brahma' [tai.u. 2.1.2]. By the word 'api,' it is implied that the primary meaning of Brahman is found there. 'aṁśakaiḥ' refers to the līlā-avatāras and guṇa-avatāras. 'pumān' is the Puruṣa, the indwelling Supersoul, known as Paramātmā. 'ekaṁ' means 'other than the one named Śrī Kṛṣṇa.' By 'yasyaiva,' it is shown that even though there is an equivalence in their divinity, it is Śrī Kṛṣṇa alone who is Svayam Bhagavān (the original Supreme Lord). The form named 'nārāyaṇākhyam' is the Lord of Śrī, the ruler of the supreme spiritual sky called Mahā-vaikuṇṭha, as described in the Pādma Purāṇa, Uttara-khaṇḍa, and other texts. The phrase 'svayaṁ bhagavān' here indicates the authority of the Śrīmad Bhāgavatam's statement 'kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam' [bhā.pu. 1.3.28]. 'śrīḥ' indicates His inseparable internal potency (svarūpa-śakti). 'iha' means 'in this world.' 'tat-pāda-bhājām' means 'of those who worship His lotus feet.' 'prema' means intense love. 'vidhattām' means 'may He grant' or 'may He cause to manifest.' ||8||
