So in this śloka, the word śraddhā is mentioned. However, the intended meaning in this verse is śraddhā-ābhāsa. It is not pure śraddhā, because if one worships Kṛṣṇa but not His devotee, then his śraddhā is either chāyā, shadow, or pratibimba. That faith is a worldly faith. It is not a spiritual faith, aprākṛta-śraddhā. Therefore, we should understand that anyone in whom we see that faith, he is a materialistic devotee, or that is prākṛta-bhakta, or the semblance of a Vaiṣṇava, vaiṣṇava-ābhāsa."

Like Śrīla Haridāsa Ṭhākura used to come, and they had a sabhā, meeting of the Vaiṣṇavas there. But Mahāprabhu called them vaiṣṇava-prāya. Prāya means 'almost', almost a Vaiṣṇava, not actually. Almost a Vaiṣṇava, prāya. Prāya means 'almost'.

Vaiṣṇava-prāya means a person, they behave like a Vaiṣṇava, but actually they're not. And viṣayī-prāya means, they behave like a sense-enjoyer, but actually they're a Vaiṣṇava. The example of viṣayī-prāya is Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi.
