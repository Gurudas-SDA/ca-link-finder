illusion (bhrama), inattention (pramāda), the desire to deceive (vipralipsā), and sensory inadequacy (karaṇāpāṭava), is the root means of knowledge. This is because the others are generally tainted by human defects like illusion, leading to erroneous perceptions, and it is impossible for humans to determine whether they are valid or fallacious, whereas testimony lacks these defects. Therefore, just as servants are rooted in the king, the other means of knowledge are rooted in testimony, but testimony itself is independent. They assist it only sometimes, according to their capacity, whereas testimony, being autonomous, is seen to operate even by overriding them. What is established by testimony cannot be contradicted by them, and for a substance untouchable by their powers, testimony alone is the most effective means of proof. For example, perception is of six types, being generated by the mind, intellect, and the five senses. Each of these is again of two types, determinate (savikalpaka) and indeterminate (nirvikalpaka), making twelve types. This is again of two types: expert and non-expert. In expert perception, there is freedom from human defects like contradiction and illusion, and testimony is also its basis. In non-expert perception, there is doubt. Its knowledge is fallible, as in 'Upon seeing an illusory head, one thinks, This is Devadatta's head."' But testimony is not like this, for example, 'There is snow in the Himalayas, there are jewels in the ocean.' Here, knowledge is rooted in testimony itself. For instance, when someone who has seen an illusory head before disbelieves even a real one out of illusion, even if he knows from a voice from the sky that 'This is real,' he cannot determine anything with certainty without serving the wise. This is the position of all logicians. The independence of testimony is seen in cases like 'You are the tenth person.' The word 'I am the tenth' immediately removes the illusion that obscures this valid knowledge, just by entering the ear, thus its independence is clear. Perception assists testimony according to its own power, as in 'Fire is the remedy for cold,' but not in 'You, my son, were in my womb in the city of Mathurā.' Testimony, however, can override perception, as with a mantra like 'There is no poison in you, though bitten by a snake.' What is established by testimony is not contradicted by perception, as in 'Calcined gold is smooth.' Testimony alone is the most effective means of proof, as in the case of planetary influences on a person's body. The position that 'what is established by everyone's perception is true' is defeated because it is impossible for everyone to gather in one place.
