Gaccha, go. In Sanskrit, the verb comes at the end of the sentence, not in the beginning. If you move the verb from the end to the beginning, it means you're making very strong emphasis. So He took the hand of Uddhava and said, "Gaccha, go! Go where?" Uddhava likes to be with Kṛṣṇa in Mathura. He doesn't want to go anywhere. "Gacchoddhava vrajaṁ saumya, go to Vraja." (..) Saumya means who is like Soma, the moon. And when you see the moon and walk in the moonlight all your cares go away, you feel happy. So Uddhava's face is like the moon, if anyone sees him, they feel very peaceful. Gacchoddhava vrajaṁ saumya pitror nau prītim āvaha. Pitṛ means parents. Prītim āvaha, "Bring some consolation, some satisfaction, some happiness to our parents." Here the word (..) nau is a possessive pronoun, means our. It's plural. Is are Nanda and Yaśodā Uddhava's parents? No, then why is He saying "our parents"? One meaning is if someone is very important, then they are allowed to use the royal we. That means plural. They speak of themselves they can use the plural if they are important. Kṛṣṇa is not thinking He is important, but when He remembered Nanda and Yaśodā He became so proud that they were His parents that He said He used the plural for Himself. You see, in each word of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam so much love is there. So when He remembered Nanda Mahārāja and Yaśodā-maiyā He became so proud, "My parents are so wonderful," that He (..) used the plural. Another meaning, "our parents" means Me and Balarāma, but that would be dual case, but this is bahuvacanam, plural: My parents, Balarāma, and your parents also. Meaning that because you have such a strong relationship with Me, therefore My parents are your parents. So it's expression of love for Uddhava also. Pitror nau prītim āvaha also means you should think that My parents are your parents because if a child goes far away, they have to go to school in another country, then the mother and father they are worried, but they know where they are, what they are doing. But if a child is kidnapped, then how are the parents worried? This anxiety is intense, very intense. So essentially Kṛṣṇa has been kidnapped. Фактически Кришну украли. He said, "I'm coming back." He just went to Mathura with His father to go for a few days and come back, but when He arrived there, then the Yadu dynasty, they kidnapped Him. And then they sent Him very far away to Ujjain. And now so many asuras, demons all over the world, having killed Kaṁsa, all the associates and the allies of Kaṁsa, they all want to kill Kṛṣṇa, so He is in great danger. So Nanda and Yaśodā are in extreme anxiety. And Kṛṣṇa wants to convey to Uddhava, "They are our parents," that Uddhava will feel the anxiety that they are feeling and be enthused to leave, because Uddhava doesn't want to leave Kṛṣṇa. So He is giving him a important mission. "You must go there, prītim āvaha, and bring some consolation to our parents." Another meaning, prītim ava nau, pitror nau, our parents: Here many people say Kṛṣṇa is the son of Vasudeva and Devakī, right? And everywhere you go, even if you go to Gokula, they say, "This is not the, Kṛṣṇa was born in the prison cell of Kaṁsa and Vasudeva Mahārāja brought him here." But what is Kṛṣṇa saying? Kṛṣṇa is supreme pramāṇa, tad-vākyaṁ tu sākṣād bhagavat-praṇītam. The Vedas come from Kṛṣṇa, what He tells is the truth, and He is saying that Nanda and Yaśodā are My parents. So He is telling Uddhava, "Go there and you be pramāṇa, and tell them Kṛṣṇa is saying, 'You are My parents.'" Because the moment Kṛṣṇa killed Kaṁsa, everyone at once knew in Mathura, "He's the son of Vasudeva and Devakī." Все сразу узнали, что он сын Деваки и Васудевы. Because almost 11 years before, 12 years before, on the wedding day of Devakī, Kaṁsa Mahārāja was driving the chariot of Devakī, and the aerial voice announced, "You fool Kaṁsa, you're driving the chariot of your sister, but you don't know the eighth child of Devakī will kill you." «Восьмой сын Деваки убьет тебя». So then, but no one knew who is the eighth child and where is he, but the moment Kṛṣṇa killed Kaṁsa, then the whole stadium who was watching the wrestling match, they all announced, "He must be the eighth child of Devakī because of the prophecy that the eighth child of Devakī would kill Kaṁsa. We've seen him kill Kaṁsa, he is the eighth child." So then Nanda Mahārāja was in Mathura, and everyone was telling him, "You are not Kṛṣṇa's father. Yaśodā is not Kṛṣṇa's mother. He's the son of Devakī." And Nanda Mahārāja, he was worried. "Does Kṛṣṇa believe them? Does Kṛṣṇa believe what everyone is saying or not?" «Верит ли Кришна тому, что говорят вокруг?» So Kṛṣṇa is saying, "Pitror nau prītim āvaha, you go there and bring solace, saying that Kṛṣṇa is saying, 'You are My parents.' Don't think I'm staying in Mathura because I've discovered now you are my foster parents and I've found my real parents. No, you pacify them and tell them Kṛṣṇa is saying, 'You are My real parents, and I will just now, very soon I will return.'" «Очень скоро Я вернусь к вам». Pitror nau prītim āvaha. Gopīnāṁ mad-viyogādhiṁ... And hey Uddhav... Here saying, "Prītim āvaha, pacify My parents by your sweet words. You are intelligent, you can say things to them which will uplift them and take them out of their suffering." But if Uddhava will say, "I am an insignificant person. «Я падшая душа...» Your parents, Nanda and Yaśodā, in separation from You, they cannot feel happiness. So (..) what can I do? I am not qualified for this mission." So Kṛṣṇa said, "Gacchoddhava, you are a festival of joy, vrajaṁ saumya, and your face is just like the moon. That means when My mother and father, they just see you, then they'll feel some relief, even if you don't say anything, only seeing you, my dear friend." Actually Uddhava looks very much like Kṛṣṇa, he has same complexion, similar eyes, and Kṛṣṇa decorated him with His own cloth, with His own fragrant garland and ornaments. So He said, "Don't worry. I'm encouraging you, if you go there, you'll be successful. Just when they see you, they'll feel some relief. Then you should speak some sweet words to them and express to them how I feel." But Kṛṣṇa said, "Gopīnāṁ mad-viyogādhiṁ." The gopīs are viyoga-ādhi, that means feeling the pain of viyoga. Yoga means meeting, viyoga means separation. Gopīs feeling the pain of separation, "Mat-sandeśair vimocaya, save them from this pain by giving My sandeśa, My messages." Here is in plural also. "My I have many messages, I will tell you. Don't," that means don't try to pacify gopīs by your intelligence. "Just repeat My exact words what I'm saying. Why? Because My parents you may to some degree understand them and think of something appropriate to say, but the love of the gopīs of Vṛndāvana is far beyond anything you can possibly imagine, and if you try to say something to pacify them, whatever you say will only make the situation worse. So I'll give you some message, just repeat these messages." It also means don't give My message to My parents, the converse is true also, because this message is specifically for the gopīs, and their love is such that they will understand, they will be able to appreciate it. But if this message you'll tell to My parents, then they'll become more distressed. So both ways: on the one hand, don't try to pacify the gopīs, say the message; on the other hand, don't tell this message to My parents. All this is included in this verse that Kṛṣṇa is telling to Uddhava. So, oh, now it is very late, mahā-rātri. So we'll continue tomorrow.
