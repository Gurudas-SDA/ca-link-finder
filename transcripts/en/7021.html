hṛdayaṁ tvad-aloka-kātaraṁ
dayita bhrāmyati kiṁ karomy aham		[Śrī Mādhavendra Purī said:] “O You whose heart is most merciful to the wretched! O Master! O Lord of Mathurā! When will I have Your darśana? O Lord of My life, because I cannot see You, My heart has become agitated. What shall I do now?”

──────────────────────────────────────────────────

How beautiful! This verse is like an ocean of rasa. Just like candana, the more you grind the candana, the more the fragrance comes. So the more the devotees remember this verse, the more prema-rasa comes from it.

[00:47:49] Śrī Kṛṣṇa has left Vṛndāvana on the chariot of Akrūra and gone to Mathurā. And Rādhikā is weeping. She said, "ayi dīna-dayārdra-nātha he"(1). Oh, Nātha, You are My Gopīnātha. That Nātha means prāṇa. "You are the prāṇa, the life and soul. My life and soul, and the life and soul of all the gopīs of Vṛndāvana. dīna-dayārdra-nātha(1), and Your heart is always melting in compassion for those persons who are suffering." So in the ocean of Rādhārāṇī's prema, when She says this first line, then the wave of sañcārī-bhāvas are coming. The first, nirveda. Nirveda means ātma-nindā. That is, Rādhārāṇī is criticizing Herself, "Oh, I am most wretched. I have no love at all, because My Śyāmasundara has gone, and I am still breathing." The next wave of emotion fighting with the first called the viṣāda. Viṣāda means anutāpa, regret. "So many dreams, and so many desires, so many services I wanted to render to Kṛṣṇa. I wanted to please Him in so many ways, but now He is gone, alas! Why did not I (..) do these things before?" Then dainya, humility, a wave of humility came. Humility means ātmani ayogya-buddhi, having the idea that I am completely unqualified. So just then, another wave of emotion came, asūyā, hostility. Hostility means when a person has all good qualities, but you look for some fault in them. In this case, Rādhikā is become angry with Kṛṣṇa. "mathurā-nātha kadāvalokyase"(1), oh, You used to be Gopīnātha, Vrajanātha, but now You became Mathurā-nātha. You have left the cowherd village and gone to the big city, and now You are acting like You're a prince. Gopas, cowherd people, are very soft, but those kṣatriyas, those warriors there, they are very hard. So by associating with them, You are not dayā-dīna-nātha. You have no soft heart now. Now You've become very cruel." But then, She became overwhelmed with eagerness, autsukya. "mathurā-nātha kadāvalokyase"(1), when will I see You again?" Then pride came. Garva, pride. A wave of pride. "dayita bhrāmyati kiṁ karomy aham"(1), dayita means, 'Oh, My darling, My beloved.'" That means, "(..) Yes, I know there are many beautiful girls in Mathurā. And they are not simple village girls like us. They are princesses from the royal family." But Rādhikā became proud. "Oh, dayita, oh, My darling, I know that I love You, and You love Me. I cannot forget You, and You cannot forget Me. So even when You meet with those girls, You are suffering only and remembering Me. bhrāmyati kiṁ karomy aham(1), bhrāmyati means I am becoming bewildered. kiṁ karomy aham(1), what shall I do now?" That means She had another wave, another sañcārī-bhāva, called vitarka. She was thinking, "Will Kṛṣṇa come here? Should I go there? What should I do?" But when separation becomes very intense, and in separation, one is about to faint, another sañcārī-bhāva comes, another ecstatic emotion comes called jāḍya. And in jāḍya, the body becomes as if inert, the mind becomes inert, and one cannot think. So She was trying to decide what to do, but that ecstasy made Her unable to think. So She says, "kiṁ karomy aham"(1)? What will I do?" You cannot think. So jāḍya occurs just before fainting, then Rādhārāṇī became unconscious. Saying this verse, Caitanya Mahāprabhu also became unconscious because He is Kṛṣṇa experiencing Rādhārāṇī's prema. So it was through this verse that Śrīla Mādhavendra Purīpāda revealed to the world the ecstatic prema of Śrīmatī Rādhikā. So on today, this disappearance day, we offer our puṣpāñjali. Śrīla Mādhavendra Purīpāda kī jaya! Tirobhāva-tithi-mahotsava kī jaya! Gaura-premānande!
