The word "dharma" comes in Sanskrit from dhṛ-dhātu, which means to hold. So, the svabhāva, the nature, which any vastu, a substance, is holding, that is its dharma. But there are two types of dharma: nitya-dharma and naimittika-dharma. Nitya-dharma is the eternal occupation of the ātmā, of the soul. And naimittika-dharma means a dharma which is has been acquired or superimposed by the contact of māyā, the material energy. The dharma of water is to be liquid. But if this liquid will go into the refrigerator... Then it becomes hard, solid, not liquid anymore. So, it now has acquired nature, that acquired nature is called nisarga. But still, the nitya-dharma of the water is present, but in a latent position. As soon as you take the water out from the refrigerator, again it becomes liquid. So similarly, the dharma of our ātmā is prema-bhakti, pure love for Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa. This is the true svabhāva, the nature of ātmā. In this world, many people, millions of people, very few are serving Bhagavān. But their nitya-dharma is not lost, only it is covered for some time by naimittika-dharma. When the ātmā comes in contact with māyā, this material world, tri-guṇa, prakṛti... Then the nature of the soul, the nitya-dharma, becomes transformed temporarily, and now the jīva does naimittika-dharma.
