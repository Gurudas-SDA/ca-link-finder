Kṛṣṇa said, "Oh beautiful young lady, just you get into my boat." "Uttiṣṭha tarau, tarau" means "in my boat". But if you take the grammar in another way, it can mean "in the tree, climb a tree". So Rādhikā She understood what He meant, but to quarrel with Him, She said, "Oh I am a girl, I have no strength for climbing trees." Kṛṣṇa said, "Śakyāt khyāni taraṇim iha." "I am not saying taru, tree, I am saying taraṇi, boat." Another word for boat, taraṇi. But taraṇi can also mean the sun. Rādhikā said, "Oh, what love do I have for the sun?" Kṛṣṇa said, "(..) (..) (..) No, varttayāṁ nau-prasaṅge." Prasaṅga means subject. And another word for boat is nau. So, "I am talking about the subject of a boat." But nau can also mean belonging to two people. And prasaṅga means subject, but saṅga can also mean association. And prasaṅga means (..) very close association. So when Kṛṣṇa said, "Nau-prasaṅge, I am talking about a boat," Rādhikā said, "How dare you? You should not suggest because there's no possibility of āvayoḥ saṅga, that means the two of us meeting very intimately." In this way, whatever came from Kṛṣṇa's mouth, Rādhārāṇī was chu-chu-chu-chu cutting everything. This is Her vāmya-bhāva, contrary mood. It is a deadly weapon of Cupid for shooting the heart of Kṛṣṇa. So at that time, then Kṛṣṇa, He was speechless. He just smiled, and by His smile He admitted He was defeated.
