Sri Prem Prayojan Prabhu: Sri Prem Prayojan - How to perceive the world

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

[00:00:07] If we had to define the nature of ancient civilization, then we could express it in terms of a healthy skepticism towards appearances. In modern life, we see everything before our eyes and we take it literally at face value. Seeing is believing. But in ancient cultures, they understood the way that we see the world is appearance only. And so, they were skeptical. Those things that appear to be offering us the prospect of happiness and peace, we often find that they become a source of misery and suffering. And those situations, that lifestyle, those practices which may seem to be austere or difficult due to the element of self-restraint, in fact elevate our consciousness and bring us the happiness that we were searching for within the appearances. So modern society is in a state of crisis because of this preoccupation with the appearances, which is a misunderstanding of the nature of reality.

[00:01:38] The ancient world had such a vision that no one object would be taken alone, but it would be seen as being something located within the totality of existence, and therein its meaning could be discovered. But in modern life, we just look at what is before us, abstracting it arbitrarily from its entire location, and think that we can understand it. This is very problematic.

[00:02:10] How does this view come about? The reason is this: that when a person engages in hedonistic activities, the contact of the fine energy of the mind with the gross physical elements causes a contraction of vision, and therefore one's view becomes blinkered; one can no longer think in terms of the totality, in terms of the whole, but one has a very narrow vision of what is directly in front of us. Therefore, the Vedas say:
