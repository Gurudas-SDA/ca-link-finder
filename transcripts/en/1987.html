'pada-kadambasya' (of the collection of words) refers to that which constitutes the Veda, the Purāṇas, the Itihāsas, etc. 'apauruṣeyatvāt' (due to being of non-human origin) - meaning not composed by a jīva, but composed by the Supreme Lord. 'abhede’pi' (even in non-difference) - even in being denoted by the word 'Veda,' 'svara-krama-bhedāt' (due to a difference in accent and sequence). The separate designation is due to the difference in accent and sequence. 'bheda-nirdeśaḥ' - the practical distinction between Veda and Purāṇa. 'svaraḥ' - the accents such as udātta, anudātta, etc. Thus, the Veda is the subject of a rule of study that is differentiated by accents like udātta. The Itihāsas and Purāṇas are not subject to a rule of study differentiated by accents; rather, their study is governed by rules like: 'O king, by hearing the Itihāsas and Purāṇas with devotion, one is freed from all sins, even the killing of a brāhmaṇa.' 'One should know the reciter to be a brāhmaṇa, and not accept one from another caste out of reverence. O king, by hearing from a reciter of another caste, one goes to hell.' 'By giving to the reciter here, one obtains the fruit of hearing.' ... and so on, according to the statements from the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa and other texts cited in works like Tithi-tattva and Kalpa-taru. This is the distinction. 'krama-bhedaḥ' - a specific sequence determined by a specific introduction and conclusion. Possessing the specific sequence called Ṛg, etc., is the basis for the application of the word 'Veda,' the delimiting factor for being the subject of a study rule with specific accents, and the delimiting factor for the prohibition of study and hearing by a śūdra. Possessing the sequence of a Purāṇa, etc., is the delimiting factor for the prohibition of study by a śūdra and the delimiting factor for the injunction of hearing. Thus, even though the Veda and Purāṇas are both of non-human origin, there is a separate designation. A collection of words that conveys a specific, single meaning, whose meaning was not previously known in the world through any other means of knowledge, and which is of non-human origin, has the quality of being 'Veda.' The Purāṇas and other texts, being narrated by the supremely merciful Lord Himself for the purpose of hearing by women, śūdras, and fallen twice-borns, subsequent to the Veda, convey the meaning understood from the Veda. Therefore, the primary function of the word 'Veda' does not apply to them, but a secondary function does. Thus, despite their difference, the Vedas, Purāṇas, and Itihāsas, denoted by the primary and secondary meanings of the word 'Veda,' form a single body of literature, because they lead to the single understanding of knowing Brahman, as per the śruti 'All the Vedas declare that state.' Despite the non-difference of the Vedas, Purāṇas, and Itihāsas, the latter are not inferior to the former. Rather, they have an equal and primary status, due to the equality of their authority, being self-evident and of non-human origin. Alternatively, the word 'Veda' has two potencies. One is in the form of the specific sequence of the Ṛg, etc., and the other, by virtue of being of non-human origin, is common to the four Vedas, the Purāṇas, and the Itihāsas. The fruit of accepting these two functions has already been stated. Here, 'Veda-purāṇam' means the removal of the expectancy raised by the Veda. It is said: 'Because of the unity of meaning, it is a single sentence, if it were divided, it would be expectant.' 'Unity of meaning' is the unity of the understanding of the intended meaning. In the case of the Veda, the intended understanding is the determination of the truth of Brahman. 'ekaṁ vākyaṁ' - one book. 'vibhāge' - even when the books are presented separately. Here, 'ākāṅkṣā' (expectancy) is the disciple's inquiry, 'After understanding the meaning of the Veda, how can this meaning be consistent, given its impossibility, etc.?' The removal of this is done by the Itihāsas and Purāṇas. Thus, the idea of the superiority of the Itihāsas and Purāṇas with respect to the Veda. The fault of redundancy in the Itihāsas and Purāṇas, given that they are a single body of literature with the Veda and serve to summarize its meaning, is dismissed because they are in the form of a commentary on the meaning of the four Vedas. ||12||
