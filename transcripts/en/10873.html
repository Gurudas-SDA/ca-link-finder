Brahmā was utterly astonished seeing the liberation of Aghāsura by the Lord, a liberation unheard of for such a being in any other incarnation or from the source of incarnations. Desiring to see more of His wonderful glories, he took the calves and cowherd boys elsewhere and disappeared. Thus he saw His further glories, as is understood from the essence of this section. And this vision of further glory was not mere imagination. By verses starting "tāvat sarve vatsa-pālāḥ" [SB 10.13.46], it is said that Śrī Kṛṣṇa, by His partial expansions, manifested and showed directly to Brahmā millions of such lords of universes, each worshipped separately by their respective potencies, opulences, the twenty-four elements, time, nature, and the universes born from them, along with their creators like Brahmā and all living beings down to the blades of grass. Such a vision must be the extraordinary seed for Svāmī's commentary on "kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam" as "He in whom all powers are manifest." The display of the universal form can be done by any of the Puruṣas who are the inner controllers of the universes. Therefore, just as he explained the Virāṭ and the Puruṣa as non-different for the sake of worship, his explanation of the non-difference between the Puruṣa and Bhagavān in verses like "jagṛhe pauruṣaṁ rūpam" is also for worship. In reality, he has accepted Śrī Kṛṣṇa as the supreme shelter, as he says: "I bow to that supreme abode named Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the shelter of the universe, which is characterized by the nine topics such as cosmic creation and recreation. In the Tenth Canto, the tenth topic, the ultimate shelter, is described as the form of both the sheltered and the shelter, playing as the supreme bliss in the ocean of the Yadu dynasty." [Bhāvārtha-dīpikā, 10.2.1] If he considered others also to be the supreme shelter, then specifying the "tenth" would be meaningless. Therefore, "Nārāyaṇa is Your limb" is well said.
