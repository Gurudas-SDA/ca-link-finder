Because their practice is largely to obscure the beginningless, unbroken Veda, and because their conduct is to destroy the beginningless varṇāśrama system, and because they establish their own communities by corrupting the varṇas with their own food and customs, it is understood to be recent. It clearly follows that this scripture was established by someone just now. But is it not seen in the Veda as well, statements like 'stones float,' 'the earth spoke,' 'the waters spoke,' which seem to indicate unreliability (i.e., falsehood)? It is replied: This is a praise to enhance the power of the stones that are part of a specific ritual (in granting the fruit of the action). And since this is famously known in the construction of the bridge by Śrī Rāma, it is appropriate, so there is no fault. Similarly, in 'the earth spoke,' 'the waters spoke,' it should be known that the presiding deities of those elements are being referred to. Thus, in every way, the Veda is reliable. But because it is the word of the omniscient Lord, it is difficult for non-omniscient living beings to understand. Its meaning can be experienced everywhere only by those who have a special perception gained by His power, not by logicians. It is said in the Puruṣottama-tantra: 'Experience combined with the meaning of the scripture is considered the highest proof. Inference and other means do not independently attain the status of proof.' The same is the opinion of the author of the Brahma-sūtras, in statements like 'tarkāpratiṣṭhānāt' (because of the inconclusiveness of logic) [bra.sū. 2.1.11], and 'śrutes tu śabda-mūlatvāt' (but from śruti, because it is based on the word) [bra.sū. 2.1.27]. And the śruti says: 'This understanding is not to be attained by logic, O dearest; when taught by another, it leads to proper knowledge' [ka.u. 1.2.9], and 'Covered by mist and babbling' [ṛk saṁ 10.82.7]. The meaning of the śruti verse is that logicians are engaged in wrangling. Therefore, in the Varāha Purāṇa: 'Inference without scripture can be made everywhere. Therefore, it is not powerful enough to perceive without scripture.' The Advaita-vādins have said: 'A conclusion reached with great effort by skilled logicians is explained otherwise by even more expert ones' [vā.pu. 1.34]. And in the Advaita-śārīraka: 'It is not possible to gather all the logicians of the past, present, and future in one place and time, by which we could accept their opinion as correct knowledge. Since the Veda is eternal and the cause of the generation of knowledge, its meaning is fixed and established. The correctness of the knowledge generated by it cannot be denied by any logician of the past, present, or future' [bra.sū. 2.1.11, śā.bhā.]. When it is seen that something is taught through logic in the scripture, that is appropriate only there, because it is in the form of scripture, and because that logic is intended only to facilitate understanding. If it were that only that Vedic statement is authoritative which can be established by logic, then let there be only logic; what is the use of the Veda? The intention everywhere is that those who abandon the Veda are outsiders. Therefore, it is said in the Mahābhārata that their destination is to become jackals [ma.bhā. 12.180.47-49]. As for the acceptance of logic called 'manana' (reflection) in statements like 'śrotavyo mantavya' [bṛ.ā.u. 2.4.5], it is said in the Kūrma Purāṇa: 'The reasoning to determine what the intended meaning is without contradiction between the beginning and the end is called tarka. One should avoid dry logic.' Now, with the authority of all Vedic statements established, some say: the authority of the Veda lies only in matters of action (kārya), not in established realities (siddhe), because its potency (śakti) and purport (tātparya) are determined only in that context. The potency is understood as follows: when an elder says to a younger person, 'Bring the cow,' a child, observing him engaged in bringing the cow, understands that the meaning of this sentence is to bring the creature with a dewlap. Thus, since from the very beginning the engagement is connected with action, the grasp of the word's potency is in that context. And so, the purport would also be there.
