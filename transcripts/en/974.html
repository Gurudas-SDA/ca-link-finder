[01:11:27] One more thing, very important. Dadṛśuḥ means they see, or na dadṛśuḥ, they do not see. Not, sorry, did not see. This is in the past tense in Sanskrit. Dadṛśuḥ means they did not see. And this, the construction in Sanskrit grammar is used for a completed action, an action which is completed. So the secret of this word, you see in each word how much rasa is there in Bhāgavata? That they did not see happiness anywhere, but not (..) now they have seen happiness. Why? Please. Why? Because Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam does not say that when Kṛṣṇa left and went to Mathurā and Dvārakā, does not say that He came back. But by Kṛṣṇa saying to Uddhava, "Dadṛśuḥ, they did not see happiness," means that they see happiness now, because at the time when I killed Dantavakra in Mathurā, I crossed the Yamunā and met with all the Vrajavāsīs, and then together with them, in another form, we all went to Goloka Vṛndāvana. (..) If Uddhava will say, "But all these years you gave them so much pain and they are there in Vṛndāvana," Kṛṣṇa is saying, "Dadṛśuḥ sukhāya. That was the situation." (..) Kṛṣṇa killed Dantavakra, He crossed the Yamunā, and met with the Vrajavāsīs in Lohavana, and enjoyed pastimes with them for some months. And then in His original form, He became aprakaṭa with all Vrajavāsīs and they entered the Goloka Vṛndāvana. They were already there, but they became aprakaṭa from this world. And in another form, He got on the chariot and went back to Dvārakā and all the Dvārakā-līlā continued. So this is being spoken by Kṛṣṇa to Uddhava at the end of His Dvārakā-līlā, but His Vraja-līlā already wound up and took to Goloka Vṛndāvana. Therefore, dadṛśuḥ sukhāya. Understand?
