By the causeless mercy of Śrī Guru and Gaurāṅga, now we have arrived at this supremely pure, transcendental holy place. Śrī Kusuma-sarovara kī jaya! When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was performing parikramā of Girirāja Govardhana, He came here and Caitanya Mahāprabhu also took bath in the lake here. It's mentioned in Caitanya-caritāmṛta as (..) Sumanaḥ-sarovara. Well, sumanaḥ and kusum are—they are synonyms. Means the sarovara, the lake of flowers. Because here this beautiful lake, it is filled with many different types of lotus flowers. It is filled with warbling swans and geese and on the forests all the way around this Kusuma-sarovara, there are beautiful trees, belī, camelī, jūhī, mādhavī, maulasarī, bakula and they're all blossoming. The specialty of this place is that even though different flowers, they blossom in different seasons, but here at Kusuma-sarovara, all the flowers of all the six seasons are always blossoming here at the same time.

[00:01:54] So, one day Śrī Kṛṣṇa, He came here. And in a full of eagerness to meet with Śrīmatī Rādhikā. So, Śrīmatī Rādhikā, She worships Sūryadeva, the sun-god. And the Sūrya temple is on this side, some few kilometers away. So, She comes here to pick flowers for the pūjā of Sūryadeva. This past-time is so full of rasa. Why? Because leaving Her home and going to pick flowers and going to worship Sūryadeva, this is all bahānā, that means an excuse. It's an excuse to get out of the home and secretly go to meet with Kṛṣṇa. So, this is very convenient that Sūryadeva is just here, Kusuma-sarovara is just here, but Rādhā-kuṇḍa is close by. So, She can give the excuse to Her mother-in-law, "I am just going. I will pick some flowers in Kusuma-sarovara and go to sur- Sūrya-kuṇḍa to worship the sun-god." And Jaṭilā, she thinks this is very wonderful. "Because if my daughter-in-law will do pūjā to the devatās, then my son will become wealthy, he'll become rich and have thousands and thousands of cows." So, on the pretext of picking flowers and worshipping the sun-god, Rādhikā comes here, but Her aim and objective is to go to Rādhā-kuṇḍa and serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa there. So, one day She came here, but Kṛṣṇa had already arrived. And He climbed into a tree that was full of beautiful flowers. And He put His foot on one branch and pushed the branch down. So, when Rādhikā was walking, "Oh! These are very beautiful. Let me pick." And She grabbed the branch with one hand and pulled and She was picking the flowers with the other and dropping them into a basket of Her sakhī. And then Śrī Kṛṣṇa, He took His foot off the branch and Rādhikā was lifted up into the air. Then, when Rādhikā was hanging, "Help! Help!" Suddenly, from nowhere a hero appeared. So, (..)
