jagau kalaṁ vāma-dṛśāṁ manoharam		Lord Kṛṣṇa saw the unbroken disk of the full moon glowing with the red effulgence of newly applied vermilion, as if it were the face of the goddess of fortune. He also saw the kumuda lotuses opening in response to the moon’s presence and the forest gently illumined by its rays. Thus the Lord began to play sweetly on His flute, attracting the minds of the beautiful-eyed gopīs.

On this night, Śukadeva Gosvāmī himself described dṛṣṭvā kumudvantam akhaṇḍa-maṇḍalam(1). Then the full moon began to shine. But who is the full moon? He is the kumuda-bandhu, the friend of the kumuda flowers, because the kumuda is the night-blooming lily. It only blossoms at the night time. And so, (..) Rādhikā is holding in Her left hand a kumuda, and it indicates that She's dancing with Kṛṣṇa in the rāsa-līlā at night, and this is the very night actually of the śāradīya-rāsa described by Śukadeva Gosvāmī in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. So, we are all most fortunate to have the darśana of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in the rāsa-līlā with Rādhikā holding the blossoming kumuda. Śrī Śrī Yugala Bihārī jīu kī jaya! So everyone can come, give praṇāma, leave some praṇāmī. Bājāo.
