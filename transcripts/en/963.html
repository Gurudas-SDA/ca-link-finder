sarva-dharmān parityajya
mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja
ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo
mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ		Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────

In Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, it is said: everyone who is born is in debt. You have debt to the devatās. They are giving you sunlight, they are giving you rain, and food, everything. They are also giving you the power of your senses. So if you don't do sacrifice to the demigods, then you're taking from them, you are a thief, taking without paying. Like if you use electricity, but you don't pay the bill, then it will be cut off. But people are getting the benefit of the sunshine and the rain, and they think, "I will not pay anything." So (..) if one will not follow dharma and worship the devatās, then you are a thief and they will give you punishment. So everyone has a debt to the devas. "Devarṣi" — everyone has a debt to the ṛṣis. That means the sages, the very educated people of the past. If in the past there were not great, educated persons, then how would you receive your education? You have not discovered everything yourself, you have learned from the past, people of the past. So you have a debt to them. "Devarṣi-bhūtāpta" — "bhūta" means all living entities. From cows, we get milk. From bees, we get honey. From trees, we get fruit. So you are dependent on them. "Āpta" means your family members. So your family members from childhood have taken care of you. And in the future, when you become old, they will take care of you. "Devarṣi-bhūtāpta-nṛṇāṁ pitṝṇām". Also the king, if there is no king or government, then there is no law and order in society. So you are so safe in your home and you are safe to walk the street because there's someone maintaining the law and order. And you are indebted to your forefathers as well, from whom you have inherited your family wealth. So there are seven shelters in this world: devas, ṛṣi, bhūta, āpta, rāja, pitṛ and so on. But Śrī Kṛṣṇa said, "No." He lifted Govardhana for seven days to tell everyone: "Give up all these shelters and just take shelter of Me."

"Only come in My shelter and I will release you from all debts. Whatever anyone else can give to you, that is from them."

[01:26:59] So Kṛṣṇa, by lifting Govardhana Hill, is reminding us, at every moment of your life, take shelter of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, by body, mind and words. And by this līlā of inviting everyone to come in His shelter, tām prema-vegena, He's also giving very powerful prema, especially to Vraja-gopīs. Go-vardhana. "Go" also means "gopī", so who nourishes the prema of gopīs is called Govardhana. In every stage of this līlā, Kṛṣṇa was actually doing everything only for Rādhārāṇī. The ordinary people think Kṛṣṇa lifted Govardhana to correct Indra's pride, but that was a secondary reason. (..) The primary reason that Kṛṣṇa does everything is because of His love for Rādhā and the gopīs. Because they're married to others, they're not allowed to look at Kṛṣṇa. Their mother-in-law's, "Hey, what are you..." But when Kṛṣṇa lifted Govardhana, then all gopīs can, if the mother-in-law say, "What are you looking at?" they'd say, "What are you looking at? Everyone is looking at..." For seven days they could drink... Also gopīs, they used to think, "Oh, my life is so bad. I have to live with my very angry husband, Abhimanyu. I'll never know what it's like to live in the same house with Kṛṣṇa." So to fulfill this desire, Kṛṣṇa lifted Govardhana: "Come, stay with Me." And so secretly that desire to live with Kṛṣṇa in the same house, it was fulfilled. So all these pastimes Kṛṣṇa is doing for love of Rādhārāṇī. So you should pray to Govardhana: "Go" means "gopī", and "vardhan" means nourish or increase. "Oh, Girirāja Govardhana, please increase the number of gopīs by one. Make one more gopī, me."
